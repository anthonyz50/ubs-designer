# Best Practice Gallery

> See the tone of voice in action across real UBS communications.

---

## Key Writing Principles (extracted from examples)

### Clear Writing
- Use contractions ('we've', 'it's') to sound like you speak
- Short sentences keep things simple
- Put the main point first
- No business speak or jargon
- Use subheadings to break up content
- Use bullet points (each starting with a verb)
- Replace formal words with everyday alternatives ('ensure' → 'make sure', 'additional' → 'extra')
- Use active verbs instead of passive ones
- Cut unnecessary phrases to reduce word count (aim for 10-25% reduction)

### Convincing Writing
- Take out hysterical exclamation marks
- Remove empty-sounding adjectives ('unique', 'exciting', 'robust', 'pivotal', 'state-of-the-art')
- Get rid of jargon ('leverage', 'solutions', 'global platform')
- Lead on benefit to users, not the process
- Put what readers care about first
- Only say what you need to

### Writing with Charm
- Draw readers in with observations or intriguing headlines
- Ask questions to keep readers interested
- Use repetition to create rhythm
- Mix sentence lengths for engagement
- Be personal: 'we' and 'us', not 'UBS'
- Paint pictures with natural-sounding phrases
- Create memorable moments with unexpected asides

## Examples by Category

### Articles and Reports
- HR communications
- Global Wealth Management publications
- Internal Communications

### Marketing Collateral
- GWM marketing materials
- Art Sponsorship materials
- Asset Management publications
- Branding instructions

### Social Media
- Facebook posts: draw readers in with observations, be personal ('we' not 'UBS')
- Twitter posts: use questions to intrigue, be personal

### Digital Communications
- Legal text: keep it simple, only say what you have to
- GWM digital: put main point first, use subheadings
- P&C Banking: use natural-sounding headlines
- Asset Management: start with observations that resonate

### Emails & Letters
- Sponsorship invitations: get right to the point, replace formal language with warm alternatives
- Out of office messages: be unexpectedly different from usual formal formula, paint a picture
- HR emails: use everyday words, see things from reader's POV
- Cookie policy: reduce from 62 to 17 words, remove jargon, use personal pronouns
