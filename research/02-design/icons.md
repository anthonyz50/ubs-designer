# Icons

> Every icon should earn its spot

## Top Takeaways

1. Thoughtfully used, icons improve usability, clarity and comprehensibility.
2. Use icons only if they serve a **clear purpose**, rather than for decorative use.
3. Our libraries offer a variety of icons, use existing designs before creating new ones.

---

## Usage

Icons are visual symbols that convey ideas or actions, often used in digital interfaces, infographics, presentations and wayfinding systems.

### Icons Enhance Your Message By:
- Helping users to quickly understand and interact, especially with digital interfaces
- Communicating meaning instantly, ideal for fast-paced, information-rich environments
- Supporting global understanding across languages and cultures
- Grabbing attention and making a message memorable

**Not every message needs an icon.** Icons should not be used to fill space or for decoration.

### Decision Framework:

#### Purpose
- Is the icon adding clarity or just filling space?
- Would the message still be clear without it?
- Use icons where the purpose is immediately recognisable
- Don't use icons where complexity would increase

#### Experience
- Does the icon help users navigate, act, or understand faster?
- Is it guiding attention or reducing cognitive load?
- Use icons as key elements for grasping and understanding information
- Don't use icons if other information (e.g. numbers) is the key element

#### Context
- Is the icon the right fit for the context?
- Use icons where the message must be reinforced in a specific context
- Don't use icons where information is clearly structured and easy to understand

#### Touchpoint
- Is the icon appropriate for the platform (e.g. app, print, signage)?
- Does it suit the audience and tone of the content?

## Types

### Illustrative Icons
Our standard icon type for the majority of applications.

#### Principles (≥ 24 px)

**Overall:**
- Open elements which are light, crisp, open and friendly in character
- Ensure a consistent line weight ratio of **1:2** where possible
- Consider the context and aim for visual balance with other icons

**Red Accents:**
- Use sparingly and in context
- Distinct red accent on icons ensures instant recognition
- Option: black illustrative icons can be used where there is too much red, where red is not appropriate or for clarity

**Variants:** Black with red accent | All black

**Character:**
- Geometric and open shapes
- Straight line ends, sharp corners mixed with rounded corners where it fits the content

**Line Weight Ratio:**
- Ratio of 1:2 with red accent always on the thicker line
- Use a thicker line purposefully, as it can change the meaning of an icon
- Line thicknesses should visually match other symbols on a page

### Web App Icons
Use web app icons for functional elements in digital interfaces, such as navigation bars, tab bars, and selection menus.

- **Sizes:** 12, 16 and 24 pixels
- **Colour:** Black only
- Don't resize icons downloaded from the library, as they are pixel-perfect
- Keep icons simple and recognisable, remove or exaggerate details for clarity
- **Safe zone:** To balance differently shaped icons
- **Open ends:** For a light look and optimistic touch

---

## Motion

Adding small movements to icons can add charm to any digital material.

- The animation should expand on the meaning of the icon
- Let the red accent appear at the end to underline the message
- Use fast pace and eased movement, but prioritise clarity
- Develop a loop for a smooth presentation

---

## Don't

- No too many ideas in one icon
- No overloaded impression
- No decorative usage
- No icons with unclear meaning / too playful
- No substitution of images with icons
- No inappropriate context
