# Data Visualization

> Let your information, facts and figures take shape in an accessible way

## Top Takeaways

1. Keep it simple by **reducing information to a minimum**.
2. Use the data to **tell a story** or make a complex concept clear.
3. Our data visualisations are **light, crisp, open and friendly** in character.

---

## Basic Principles

### Figures
The simplest way to show twelve percent: **12%**

### Charts and Tables
Visualising quantities helps us understand relationships and change. Reduce information to a minimum.

### Infographics
Tell a story or make a complex concept clear. Highlight the main point with our distinctive style for lines and shapes.

---

## 6-Step Process

### 1. Follow the Strategy
- **Content:** Determine valuable points, reduce complexity, dig deeper to contextualise
- **Story:** Search for the narrative the audience would care about, focus on data density
- **Message:** Refine the message, determine what should be emphasised, focus on impact and clarity

### 2. Keep It Simple
- Use as few graphic elements as possible
- Focus on the data, not on the graphics
- Only add elements (grid lines etc.) where it helps communicate
- Reduce visual impact of secondary elements

### 3. Talk Directly to the Audience
- Start with a question or informative headline
- What is the intended message? Who is the audience? What is the medium?

### 4. Highlight the Insight
- Point to an insight with an **Insight Flag (red arrow)**
- Write a summary in plain language
- Highlight important elements with colour

### 5. Create the Basis with Essential Elements
- **Typography:** Frutiger Light (standard), Frutiger Bold or Frutiger Light Condensed. Don't mix within one visualisation.
- **Colours:** Be aware red may have negative association. Ensure clarity and contrast.
- **Lines:** Adjust line weights to harmonise with Frutiger Light. Ratio 1:2 for icons.
- **Shapes:** Straight ends, sharp corners, rounded corners where context fits.
- **Arrows:** Angles maximum 90 degrees. Line weight 1:2.
- **Background:** Pay attention to contrast ratio for accessibility.

### 6. Use the Possibilities of Emphasis
Colour, Contrast, Stroke contrast, Scale, Proximity, Negative space, Repetition, Dashed/dotted lines, Big numbers

## Charts

### Dimension
Charts are always **2D** for clarity through simplicity.

### Circular
Prefer **donut charts** over pie charts for space and elegance.

### Gap
Use a gap between colour blocks:
- **Print:** 1.5 point
- **Screen:** 2 pixels

### Labelling
- Use clear labels with accessible text colour
- Inside colour blocks when possible
- Outside with connector lines when labels don't fit

### Highlighting
**UBS Red** is the highlight colour. When using UBS Red, don't use Bordeaux I. Use next darker shades.

### Line Types
Use different line types for complex line charts and monochrome sequences (accessibility).

### Line Ratio
Sufficient contrast between divider lines, chart lines, and anchor points.

---

## Colour Sequences

### Monochrome (standard)
One colour + UBS Red for highlights.

### Polychrome (standard)
Gray III-VI and Black (up to 5 colour blocks) + UBS Red for highlights. Light to dark arrangement.

### Multichrome (additional)
Gray III-VI + Black, Bordeaux I-III, Bronze I-III (mixed). Up to 9 colour blocks.

### Complex Charts (additional, special cases)
Additional colour palette for up to 20 segments. Use with caution. Defined colour sequence order. No other sequences should be used alongside.

## Infographics

### Approach:
- Transform information into a story or process
- Consider hierarchy and flow
- Focus on clarity
- Apply a question as the title
- Know the audience and the purpose

### Design:
- Fit the overall look and feel
- Use white space and simple visuals (outlines in corporate or gray colours)
- Use defined icon style where it makes sense
- Reduce details as much as possible
- Organise left to right, top to bottom
- Black with red accent standard for icons

### Maps
- Reduce as much as possible
- Use geographic/cultural landmarks for context

### Organisation Charts
- Ensure legibility, keep simple
- Colour can help identify groups
- Ensure clarity for black and white copies
- Available in Single Gray, Multiple Gray, Multiple Bordeaux, Multiple Bronze

## Digital Applications
- **Complex data visualisation:** In-depth analysis with data density
- **Bite-size content:** Attention-grabbing insights using established brand elements
- **Interactivity:** More detail on demand, animated transitions

## Social Media
- Icons and illustrations most appropriate (limited space)
- Use established brand elements for instant recognition
- Start with surprising/intriguing question
- Simple, clean graphics in corporate colours

## Motion and Video
- **Easing:** Changes of acceleration for realism. Avoid abrupt starts/stops.
- **Purposeful direction:** Straight, concise paths. Avoid curved/organic.
- **Transitions:** Add meaning, not embellishment. Apply opacity, scale, colour and position thoughtfully.
- **Pace:** Consistent timing. Define smallest duration and multiply for longer.
- **Contextualise:** Include relevant visualisation in transition animations.

---

## Don't

- No non-UBS icons
- No shadows
- No dominant highlight colour
- No mixed colour directions
- No gradients
- No angles
- No rounded shapes (where inappropriate)
- No non-corporate/non-secondary colours
- No 3D
- No wrong contrast ratio
